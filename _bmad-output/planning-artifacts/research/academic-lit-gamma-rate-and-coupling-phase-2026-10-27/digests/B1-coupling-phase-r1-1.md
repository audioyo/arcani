## Synthesis for the decision

- Endogenous evidence does not give a single "correct" theta phase. Human hippocampal data show encoding-related PAC with a biphasic or trough-leaning preference at slow theta (Lega 2016, phase detail not fully verified), and opposite theta phases for encoding versus retrieval (Saint Amour di Chanaz 2023). Frontal cortex during cognitive control shows gamma near the theta peak, about 0 to 60 deg (Smith 2015, via Turi 2020). Preferred phase appears to depend on region and task.
- Endogenous "trough" or "peak" labels refer to recorded LFP phase, whose polarity depends on recording site and reference. They do not map directly onto the peak of an audio envelope.
- Stimulation evidence leans toward peak-coupled over trough-coupled: peak-coupled helped spatial WM (Alekseichuk 2016) and motor learning (Akkad 2021, replicated in a pre-registered experiment vs sham); trough-coupled impaired cognitive control (Turi 2020) and was null for motor learning (Akkad 2021).
- That lean is weaker than it looks: the direct peak-vs-trough contrast in Akkad 2021 was not significant (p = 0.162); peak-coupled had no behavioral effect in Turi 2020; one 2024 study found peak-coupled reduced WM accuracy by 2.7%. The WM benefit of peak-coupling rests on one lab and has no direct replication found in this run.
- All phase-comparison evidence is electrical (tACS) and the "peak" is defined on the delivered current waveform. None of it shows that an acoustic envelope peak produces the same neural phase relation; auditory pathway latency would shift any such mapping (unverified in this run).
- No study found compared peak-coupled, trough-coupled and uncoupled gamma delivered by sound. Audio evidence found is gamma-only (2025) or theta-phase closed-loop without gamma (2024 preprint).
- Turi 2020 provides a usable control design: the same theta and gamma components superimposed without phase specificity ("uncoupled control"), plus a continuous AM variant. This matches the "uncoupled as a control" option.
- For a sound product, the evidence supports making coupling phase a recipe parameter with peak (0 deg on the theta reference envelope) as the most-cited stimulation default and uncoupled as the control; it does not support any claim that the chosen phase produces a cognitive outcome.
