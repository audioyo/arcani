## Synthesis for the decision

- The human auditory ASSR is largest near 40 Hz in awake adults (Galambos 1981; Picton 2003 review; Ross 2000). This is the most replicated finding in the set and the strongest anchor for a default.
- The response does not vanish away from 40 Hz: MEG shows separable responses up to 98 Hz, with amplitude falling as rate rises (Ross 2000), and responses near 80 Hz are robust but brainstem-weighted and less sensitive to arousal (Picton 2003). So a range wider than 40 Hz is physiologically reachable, but entrainment strength per unit of sound is expected to drop above about 50 Hz. The exact fall-off slope was not retrieved and must be read from Ross 2000 before it is used to set an upper bound.
- Individual peak (IGF) frequencies cluster around 37 to 48 Hz in adult group means (Bolland 2025 review; Yokota 2025 mean 45.6 Hz, SD 5.3). One unverified report gives a range up to about 90 Hz. A range of roughly 30 to 60 Hz would cover about plus or minus 2 to 3 SD of the one measured IGF distribution read here.
- Human auditory behavioral evidence is overwhelmingly at 40 Hz (about 58 of 62 studies in Bolland 2025). 60 Hz has one lab's results (Manippa et al., two papers) with a narrow effect (fewer recall intrusions) and no recall or working-memory gain. 80 Hz has one study. No other rate has a replicated auditory behavioral outcome. Single-session effects in healthy adults are inconsistent even at 40 Hz.
- Perception: at the asper reference (1 kHz carrier) roughness peaks near 70 Hz. For carriers of a few hundred Hz the roughness peak shifts lower, into roughly the 20 to 50 Hz region (Eddins 2015 re-plotting Fastl and Zwicker). So for this engine's carriers, rates near 40 Hz may sound close to maximally rough. This is a comfort trade-off against ASSR strength, not a safety limit.
- Carrier matters for ASSR size: at 40 Hz AM, lower carriers (250 Hz) gave substantially larger MEG responses than higher carriers (Ross et al. 2003, snippet). Picton 2003 also reports carrier-dependent phase delay. This supports keeping gamma carriers in the low hundreds of Hz, subject to the carrier-at-least-4x-rate rule.
- Long-listening tolerance: the only data found are mild adverse events (headache, dizziness, tinnitus) in chronic clinical protocols and one safety/acceptability study at 40 Hz in older adults whose results were not read. No annoyance-vs-rate data were found.
- Evidence-supported candidate, stated as a proposal for review rather than a conclusion: default 40 Hz; locked range roughly 30 to 60 Hz, with 60 Hz as an upper edge backed only by one lab, and anything above 60 Hz (up to 80 Hz) lacking auditory behavioral support beyond ASSR recordability.

## Leads not chased

- Ross et al. 2000 full text (JASA 108:679): the actual amplitude-vs-rate curve, 10 to 98 Hz, to quantify fall-off either side of 40 Hz.
- Dickinson et al. 2015 (IGF range 30.27 to 89.66 Hz, N 33): verify modality (auditory vs visual) and method.
- Cognito Therapeutics audiovisual 40 Hz trials (e.g. OVERTURE, Hajos et al. 2024; Chan et al. 2022): audiovisual, so would not count as auditory-only support; not retrieved.
- Tada et al. (60 Hz) and Shahmiri et al. (80 Hz), cited in Bolland 2025.
- The Japan 40 Hz AM safety/acceptability study (PMC12564345): carrier, depth, level, adverse events, dropout.
- "Shaping gamma oscillations through sensory stimulation: a systematic review in healthy adults" (ScienceDirect S0301051126000244, Biological Psychology, 2026): may cover non-40 rates.
- Manippa 2026 (J Alzheimer's Dis., doi:10.1177/13872877251407709), "Gamma sensory stimulation: investigating human brain response to optimize clinical translation".
- Picton, John et al. multiple-ASSR work on carrier-to-rate effects at 80 Hz; John and Picton on modulation depth.
- Light-flicker and tACS gamma studies: not chased by design. Manippa 2024 Psychophysiology includes a tACS arm (electrical), which must not be counted as auditory support.
