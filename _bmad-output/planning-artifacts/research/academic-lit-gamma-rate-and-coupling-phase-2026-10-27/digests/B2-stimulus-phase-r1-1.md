## Synthesis for the decision

- **Phase is better thought of as a delay.** In the 4 to 8 Hz range, the best-characterized EEG/MEG studies find that cortical envelope tracking behaves like a fixed time delay. Phase lag grows linearly with rate (Zou 2021; Wang 2012 as cited). So the phase offset between "peak in the sound" and "peak in the brain" is not one constant: it changes with the theta rate.
- **Size of the delay.** Scalp EEG/MEG group delays cluster around 130 to 160 ms (131/147 ms MEG AM tones; 156 ± 50 ms EEG speech). As an illustrative calculation, not a measured value: 150 ms is about 216 deg of a 4 Hz cycle and about 432 deg (that is, 72 deg wrapped) of an 8 Hz cycle. The unreported intercept would add a further constant offset.
- **Intracranial delays are shorter and more variable.** In auditory cortex, theta-band lag is 78 ± 64 ms (Lopez-Madrona 2026, preprint). Lag depends on the recording site and the measure, so there is no single "brain phase" to target.
- **Lag varies across people.** The ± 50 ms spread across 15 listeners and the ± 64 ms spread across SEEG contacts mean a single stimulus phase will land at different neural phases in different listeners. Henry & Obleser 2012 found the behaviorally optimal stimulus phase differed by listener (abstract level).
- **Evoked or oscillator is unresolved.** Zou 2021 favors evoked responses; Doelling 2019 favors an oscillator; Zoefel 2018 frames it as an open question. Either way, for a continuous steady AM tone the practical prediction is the same: neural theta follows the sound with a delay. Only the oscillator view predicts extra predictive alignment that could shift the phase (Lakatos 2013 as cited).
- **No study found tests synthetic theta-gamma coupled sound.** No study was found that presents a synthetic sound (gamma AM or click train whose depth follows a theta envelope at a chosen coupling phase) and measures brain PAC or behavior.
- **Natural speech is the closest analog.** Speech already has gamma envelope power peaking near the theta envelope peak (about 0 deg), and the auditory cortex PAC it produces looks stimulus-driven (Lopez-Madrona 2026, preprint). This suggests brain PAC under such sounds mostly mirrors the stimulus coupling, shifted by band-specific delays. Theta lags more than gamma (78 vs 59 ms), so the neural coupling phase would differ slightly from the acoustic one.
- **Gamma ASSR versus theta phase.** Evidence that gamma ASSR amplitude depends on theta phase is indirect and weak. It comes from electrical theta stimulation (tACS), not acoustic theta. In that study theta tACS reduced the 40 Hz ASSR and slightly increased theta-gamma PAC (n=23, small effects).
- **Practical implication (inference, not a finding).** Specify the coupling phase in terms of the sound's envelope, which is measurable and reproducible. Do not claim it equals a cortical phase. Any mapping to "brain phase" would be rate-dependent and listener-dependent, with an expected delay of very roughly 80 to 160 ms.

## Leads not chased

- Wang Y et al. 2012 J Neurophysiol (MEG AM tone group delay): read the original for n, rates and spread across subjects.
- Doelling et al. 2019 full text: rates used, measured phase lags, and the evoked/oscillator comparison per rate.
- Lakatos et al. 2013 (Neuron) on predictive phase alignment after stream offset.
- Ding & Simon (2012/2013) MEG TRF latencies (M50/M100-like peaks at about 50 and 100 ms).
- Luo & Poeppel 2012 MEG study with synthetic stimuli modulated at three timescales (theta + gamma ITC): closest to a synthetic nested-stimulus design.
- Lizarazu et al. 2019: confirm the journal, n, speech rates and coupling-phase shift.
- Haegens & Zion Golumbic 2018 (Neurosci Biobehav Rev), "Rhythmic facilitation of sensory processing": not retrieved.
- van Bree et al. 2021 (PLoS Biol), "Sustained neural rhythms reveal endogenous oscillations supporting speech perception": entrainment echoes.
- "Strength of Low-Frequency EEG Phase Entrainment to External Stimuli" (eNeuro 2024/2025) may quantify across-subject phase consistency.
- 2026 personalized audiovisual gamma stimulation study (PubMed 42027686): it reportedly found theta-gamma coupling, but the full record was blocked by a CAPTCHA.

## Looked for but not found

- No peer-reviewed or preprint study was found that builds a synthetic theta-envelope-modulated gamma AM or click-train stimulus with a chosen coupling phase and then measures EEG/MEG PAC or behavior.
- No study was found that directly reports gamma (40 Hz) ASSR amplitude binned by the phase of an acoustically entrained theta oscillation.
- No reported intercept (constant phase offset) for the theta-band envelope-to-EEG linear phase fit.
- No study was found that reports how consistent the stimulus-to-brain theta lag is within one person across sessions or across rates (only the across-subject spread).
- The Zoefel et al. 2018 review gives no expected stimulus-to-neural phase lag value.
