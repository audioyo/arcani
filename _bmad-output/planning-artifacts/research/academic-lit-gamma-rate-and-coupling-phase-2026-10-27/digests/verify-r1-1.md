## Corrections the original claims need

1. **C3 title:** the published title is "Spatial Working Memory in Humans Depends on Theta and **High** Gamma Synchronization in the Prefrontal Cortex". The brief drops "High".
2. **C3 trough wording:** the abstract calls trough-coupled bursts and low-gamma protocols "ineffective". Write "did not improve". Do not write "impaired" unless the full text is checked and supports it.
3. **C3 n:** not confirmed. Do not cite a sample size until it is read from the full text.
4. **C2 stimulus:** Yokota's IGF came from click chirps (alternating-polarity noise bursts, 30 to 60 Hz) embedded in music, not from AM tones. Peak-frequency estimates depend on the method: Parciauskaite et al. found about 42 Hz with a range of 36 to 53 Hz. The "roughly 37 to 48 Hz" cluster appears in an npj Aging systematic review snippet (doi via https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-025-00305-1) as a range of study-level optima, not individual peaks.
5. **C4:** "trough" is correct for the memory-related coupling sites. The hippocampal phase preference during encoding is described as biphasic, so do not reduce it to a single trough phase for the hippocampus.
6. **C5:** "Luo & Poeppel 2012" is not a nested-AM study. Its stimuli were concatenated FM segments at theta and gamma timescales. Cite it only as timescale evidence.
7. **C1:** the 1993 study puts the maximum at "30 or 40 Hz", so the peak is near 40 Hz but not sharply at 40 Hz. No ratios for 60 or 80 Hz relative to 40 Hz were retrieved. A search summary of a patent document described a minimum near 60 to 70 Hz in awake adults; that is not peer-reviewed and was not used for the verdict.
